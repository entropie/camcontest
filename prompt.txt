Compare these three snapshots from the same camera in chronological order,
three seconds apart. Describe the clearest activity you can observe from
any one dog. Follow each dog by its appearance and location. A single
visible dog is enough to score the sequence.

Use this scale:

1 — Resting. The dog stays lying in the same position across all three
    frames. A head held up throughout still counts as resting.
2 — Slight movement while lying down, such as a small head turn.
3 — Clear head movement while lying down: the dog lifts or lowers its
    head, or looks in different directions across the frames.
4 — The dog adjusts its body while staying down: shifts its weight,
    rolls onto its other side, or changes its lying position.
5 — Unclear. No dog is clearly visible, or the evidence is too ambiguous
    to distinguish rest from activity. Briefly say what is unclear.
6 — Informational. The dog sits or stands in the same place, or clearly
    gets up or lies down there.
7 — The dog takes a few steps or turns around on its resting spot.
8 — The dog walks to a different part of the room.
9 — The dog keeps moving between locations across both frame intervals.
10 — The dog shows rapid, substantial movement across the room, such as
     clearly running.

For multiple dogs, score the highest activity you can clearly observe
in a single dog. Use 5 only when you cannot give a meaningful assessment.
A dog disappearing behind another dog or out of view is not by itself
evidence of fast movement.

Describe what changed and where, for example "on the sofa" or "by the
door". If the posture stays the same, describe it as such. Keep the
description specific to these few seconds; avoid guesses about mood,
health, or how long the behavior has lasted.
